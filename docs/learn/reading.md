# Further reading

A short, annotated selection to go beyond this course. The reference lists
at the end of each chapter give the primary sources of every method.

## Textbooks

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- Turner, M. G., & Gardner, R. H. (2015). *Landscape Ecology in Theory and Practice: Pattern and Process* (2nd ed.). Springer.
  The standard graduate textbook: pattern, process, scale, neutral models, metrics, disturbance and management. Start here.
- Gergel, S. E., & Turner, M. G. (Eds.) (2017). *Learning Landscape Ecology: A Practical Guide to Concepts and Techniques* (2nd ed.). Springer.
  Laboratory exercises on scale, metrics, neutral models and connectivity; a hands-on companion to the previous book.
- Forman, R. T. T. (1995). *Land Mosaics: The Ecology of Landscapes and Regions*. Cambridge University Press.
  The patch–corridor–matrix model and the spatial processes of land transformation, with a planning perspective.
- Fletcher, R., & Fortin, M.-J. (2018). *Spatial Ecology and Conservation Modeling: Applications with R*. Springer.
  Spatial statistics, scale, habitat selection, connectivity and populations, with worked examples.
- Fortin, M.-J., & Dale, M. R. T. (2005). *Spatial Analysis: A Guide for Ecologists*. Cambridge University Press.
  The statistics of spatial pattern: autocorrelation, point patterns, surfaces and boundaries.
- Crooks, K. R., & Sanjayan, M. (Eds.) (2006). *Connectivity Conservation*. Cambridge University Press.
  Concepts, methods and case studies of connectivity for conservation.

</div>

## Key papers by topic

### Foundations and scale

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- Turner, M. G. (1989). Landscape ecology: the effect of pattern on process. *Annual Review of Ecology and Systematics*, 20, 171–197.
  The paper that set the pattern–process agenda.
- Wiens, J. A. (1989). Spatial scaling in ecology. *Functional Ecology*, 3(4), 385–397.
- Levin, S. A. (1992). The problem of pattern and scale in ecology. *Ecology*, 73(6), 1943–1967.
- Jackson, H. B., & Fahrig, L. (2012). What size is a biologically relevant landscape? *Landscape Ecology*, 27, 929–941.
- Metzger, J. P. (2001). O que é ecologia de paisagens? *Biota Neotropica*, 1(1–2).
  A concise introduction in Portuguese to the two traditions of landscape ecology.

</div>

### Habitat loss and fragmentation

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- Fahrig, L. (2003). Effects of habitat fragmentation on biodiversity. *Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics*, 34, 487–515.
  The distinction between habitat loss and fragmentation *per se*.
- Fahrig, L. (2017). Ecological responses to habitat fragmentation per se. *Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics*, 48, 1–23; and Fletcher, R. J., Jr., et al. (2018). Is habitat fragmentation good for biodiversity? *Biological Conservation*, 226, 9–15.
  Read together: the two sides of the debate.
- Haddad, N. M., et al. (2015). Habitat fragmentation and its lasting impact on Earth's ecosystems. *Science Advances*, 1(2), e1500052.
- Ribeiro, M. C., Metzger, J. P., Martensen, A. C., Ponzoni, F. J., & Hirota, M. M. (2009). The Brazilian Atlantic Forest: how much is left, and how is the remaining forest distributed? Implications for conservation. *Biological Conservation*, 142(6), 1141–1153.
  A landmark application of patch, edge and connectivity analyses to a whole biome.

</div>

### Conservation planning

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- Margules, C. R., & Pressey, R. L. (2000). Systematic conservation planning. *Nature*, 405, 243–253.
  The framework: explicit goals, gap analysis, complementary selection, implementation.
- Moilanen, A., Wilson, K. A., & Possingham, H. P. (Eds.) (2009). *Spatial Conservation Prioritization: Quantitative Methods and Computational Tools*. Oxford University Press.
  The methods and software of spatial prioritization, Zonation and Marxan among them, by their authors.
- Kukkala, A. S., & Moilanen, A. (2013). Core concepts of spatial prioritisation in systematic conservation planning. *Biological Reviews*, 88(2), 443–464.
  Complementarity, irreplaceability, efficiency and the other concepts, defined and related.
- Moilanen, A., Lehtinen, P., Kohonen, I., Jalkanen, J., Virtanen, E. A., & Kujala, H. (2022). Novel methods for spatial prioritization with applications in conservation, land use planning and ecological impact avoidance. *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*, 13(5), 1062–1072.
  Zonation 5: the iterated ranking, its rules and uses.

</div>

### Species distributions

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- Franklin, J. (2010). *Mapping Species Distributions: Spatial Inference and Prediction*. Cambridge University Press.
  A textbook of the whole workflow, from data to evaluation, by an ecologist and geographer.
- Peterson, A. T., Soberón, J., Pearson, R. G., Anderson, R. P., Martínez-Meyer, E., Nakamura, M., & Araújo, M. B. (2011). *Ecological Niches and Geographic Distributions*. Princeton University Press.
  The niche-centred view: the BAM diagram, the accessible area, environmental and geographic space.
- Guisan, A., Thuiller, W., & Zimmermann, N. E. (2017). *Habitat Suitability and Distribution Models: With Applications in R*. Cambridge University Press.
  Concepts and practice, with worked examples of every step.
- Elith, J., & Leathwick, J. R. (2009). Species distribution models: ecological explanation and prediction across space and time. *Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics*, 40, 677–697.
  The review to start with.
- Elith, J., Phillips, S. J., Hastie, T., Dudík, M., Chee, Y. E., & Yates, C. J. (2011). A statistical explanation of MaxEnt for ecologists. *Diversity and Distributions*, 17(1), 43–57.
  What Maxent estimates and how its settings act, without the black box.
- Roberts, D. R., et al. (2017). Cross-validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure. *Ecography*, 40(8), 913–929.
  Why random cross-validation is optimistic, and what to do instead.
- Valavi, R., Guillera-Arroita, G., Lahoz-Monfort, J. J., & Elith, J. (2022). Predictive performance of presence-only species distribution models: a benchmark study with reproducible code. *Ecological Monographs*, 92(1), e01486.
  Which algorithms and settings work, compared on species from six regions of the world.
- Zurell, D., et al. (2020). A standard protocol for reporting species distribution models. *Ecography*, 43(9), 1261–1277.
  The ODMAP protocol: what a paper must say about data, model, assessment and prediction.

</div>

### Landscape metrics

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- McGarigal, K. (2015). *FRAGSTATS Help*. University of Massachusetts, Amherst.
  The definitions and interpretation of every classic metric; the reference manual behind most metric names.
- Li, H., & Wu, J. (2004). Use and misuse of landscape indices. *Landscape Ecology*, 19, 389–399.
- Cushman, S. A., McGarigal, K., & Neel, M. C. (2008). Parsimony in landscape metrics: strength, universality, and consistency. *Ecological Indicators*, 8(5), 691–703.
- Hesselbarth, M. H. K., Sciaini, M., With, K. A., Wiegand, K., & Nowosad, J. (2019). landscapemetrics: an open-source R tool to calculate landscape metrics. *Ecography*, 42, 1648–1657.

</div>

### Morphology

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- Soille, P., & Vogt, P. (2009). Morphological segmentation of binary patterns. *Pattern Recognition Letters*, 30(4), 456–459.
- Vogt, P., & Riitters, K. (2017). GuidosToolbox: universal digital image object analysis. *European Journal of Remote Sensing*, 50(1), 352–361.
  The toolbox whose pattern, fragmentation and restoration methods Landschaft reproduces.

</div>

### Connectivity

<div class="ls-refs" markdown>

- Taylor, P. D., Fahrig, L., Henein, K., & Merriam, G. (1993). Connectivity is a vital element of landscape structure. *Oikos*, 68(3), 571–573.
- Saura, S., & Pascual-Hortal, L. (2007). A new habitat availability index to integrate connectivity in landscape conservation planning. *Landscape and Urban Planning*, 83(2–3), 91–103.
- Saura, S., & Rubio, L. (2010). A common currency for the different ways in which patches and links can contribute to habitat availability and connectivity in the landscape. *Ecography*, 33(3), 523–537.
- McRae, B. H., Dickson, B. G., Keitt, T. H., & Shah, V. B. (2008). Using circuit theory to model connectivity in ecology, evolution, and conservation. *Ecology*, 89(10), 2712–2724.
- Zeller, K. A., McGarigal, K., & Whiteley, A. R. (2012). Estimating landscape resistance to movement: a review. *Landscape Ecology*, 27, 777–797.
- Correa Ayram, C. A., Mendoza, M. E., Etter, A., & Salicrup, D. R. P. (2016). Habitat connectivity in biodiversity conservation: a review of recent studies and applications. *Progress in Physical Geography*, 40(1), 7–37.

</div>
